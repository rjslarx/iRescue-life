Post-adoption compliance is historically the biggest "black hole" in animal rescue. Once the dog leaves the building, shelter staff are usually too overwhelmed with new intakes to chase down a new owner for a spay/neuter receipt.
Automating this is one of the highest-value features your software offers. You want to divide your Adopter Compliance Engine into two specific tracks: Legal/Medical Compliance and Retention (Return Prevention).
Here is the master checklist for what your system should monitor and the exact automated notifications it should send to adopters:
Track 1: Legal & Medical Compliance (The "Must-Dos")
This track protects the rescue legally and ensures the animal is receiving required veterinary care.
1. Spay/Neuter Enforcement (The #1 Priority)
 * The Rule: Many rescues adopt out puppies at 8 weeks old with a legally binding contract stating the dog must be altered by 6 months of age.
 * The Notification: 14 days before the contract's spay_neuter_due_date, the system texts/emails the adopter: "Hi [Name]! A friendly reminder from the rescue that [Animal]'s spay/neuter procedure is required by [Date]. Please tap here to upload a photo of your vet receipt: [Portal Link]"
 * The Monitoring: If the date passes and no document is uploaded, it triggers a red "Compliance Breach" alert on the Shelter Manager's Daily Briefing dashboard so they can call the adopter.
2. Annual Vaccine Reminders
 * The Rule: Adopters often forget when their pet's 1-Year Rabies or DHPP/FVRCP vaccines expire.
 * The Notification: 11 months after the pet's last recorded annual vaccines, the system sends: "Hi [Name]! Can you believe it's almost been a year? This is a reminder that [Animal] will be due for their annual vet checkup and Rabies vaccine next month!"
3. Microchip Registration Verification
 * The Rule: A microchip is useless if the adopter never registers their phone number to it.
 * The Notification: 7 days post-adoption: "Hi [Name]! Please ensure you have registered [Animal]'s microchip ([Microchip Number]) with [Brand]. This is critical if they ever get lost!"
Track 2: Retention Monitoring (The "3-3-3 Rule")
This track is designed to prevent "bounce backs" (when an adopter returns a dog because they are overwhelmed). The industry standard for rescue dog decompression is the "3-3-3 Rule" (3 Days, 3 Weeks, 3 Months).
1. The 3-Day Check-in (The Overwhelm Phase)
 * The Notification: "Hi [Name]! It's been 3 days! [Animal] is likely feeling overwhelmed as they decompress. Remember to keep things quiet, positive, and stick to a routine. Need advice? Reply HELP to connect with our behavior team."
 * The Monitoring: If they reply HELP, Twilio routes this directly to your staff's dashboard so you can intervene before they give up and return the dog.
2. The 3-Week Check-in (The Routine Phase)
 * The Notification: "Happy 3 Weeks! [Animal] should be settling into your daily routine and showing their true personality now. We'd love to see how they are doing—reply with a photo!"
3. The 3-Month Check-in (The Anniversary Phase)
 * The Notification: "Happy 3-Month Rescue Anniversary to [Animal]! Thank you for giving them a forever home. 🐾"
How the Staff Monitors This (The Dashboard Loop)
For the software to work, the rescue staff needs a clean place to monitor these actions.
When an adopter uses their Adopter Portal to upload a Spay/Neuter vet receipt, the system should change the status in your adopter_compliance table to pending_review.
On the main Command Center, the staff sees an alert: "Review Spay/Neuter Proof for Barnaby." They click it, look at the uploaded image of the vet receipt, click "Approve", and the compliance loop is officially closed.
