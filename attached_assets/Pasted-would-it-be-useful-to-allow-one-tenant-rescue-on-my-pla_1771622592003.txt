Short Answer: Yes. In fact, this could be the single most powerful "Growth Hack" for your entire software platform.

In the animal welfare world, rescues transfer animals between organizations constantly. A crowded municipal shelter in Texas might transfer a litter of puppies to a foster-based rescue in Colorado. A general rescue might transfer a pregnant dog to a specialized whelping rescue.

Right now, when this happens, the sending rescue prints out 15 pages of PDF medical records, and the receiving rescue pays a staff member to sit at a desk and manually type all that data into their own software.

If you build a "Tenant-to-Tenant Transfer" feature, you eliminate hours of data entry. More importantly for you as the software founder, you create a Network Effect: Rescue A will actively tell Rescue B to sign up for irescue.life just so they can send them dogs digitally.

Here is exactly how you should architect this feature to ensure it is secure and legally compliant.

1. The "Handshake" Protocol (Consent)
You cannot let Rescue A just dump an animal into Rescue B's database without permission. It must be a two-way street.

The Request: Rescue A clicks "Transfer to Network Partner." They select Rescue B from a dropdown of irescue.life tenants and click Send.

The Inbox: Rescue B gets an alert in their Unified Triage Inbox: "Incoming Transfer: Rescue A wants to transfer Barnaby."

The Action: Rescue B reviews the medical file and clicks [ Accept ] or [ Reject ].

2. The Golden Rule: Clone, Don't Move
This is the most critical database rule for multi-tenant architecture. When Rescue B clicks [ Accept ], you must NOT simply change the tenant_id on Barnaby's existing database row from Rescue A to Rescue B.

If you do that, Barnaby disappears from Rescue A's historical records, and their end-of-year intake/outcome statistics will be ruined.

The Fix: You must create a Deep Clone of the animal. Rescue B gets a brand new row in their database for Barnaby, but you copy over the name, breed, age, and the medical_treatments history.

The Status Change: Rescue A's original version of Barnaby gets his adoption_status permanently changed to 'transferred_out'. He becomes a read-only historical archive for them.

3. Data Segregation: What Goes and What Stays?
When you clone the record, you have to be very careful about what data crosses the tenant boundary.

What TRANSFERS (The Dog's Baggage):

Core stats (Name, weight, microchip number).

All verified medical records (Vaccines, spay/neuter, past prescriptions).

Behavioral tags (e.g., 'Dog Reactive', 'Flight Risk').

What STAYS (The Rescue's Baggage):

Internal staff notes (e.g., "Barnaby's original owner was a jerk").

Foster parent personal information (Rescue B does not get Rescue A's volunteer phone numbers).

Financial data (How much Rescue A paid for his vet bills).
