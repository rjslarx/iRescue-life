To make this work flawlessly in Replit, you should divide these statuses into two categories in your code: Active and Terminal.
Here is the breakdown of how your system should treat them:
1. The "Active" Statuses (The Daily Grind)
These are animals that are currently in your custody. They need to show up on daily task lists, feeding charts, and medical dashboards.
 * Available: Shows on the public website. Ready to go.
 * Adoption Pending: Hidden from the public website. Awaiting trial completion or contract signing.
 * Transfer Pending: Hidden from the public website. Awaiting transport to a partner.
 * Stray Hold: Hidden from the public website (or sent to a specific "Found Dogs" page). This is a legal countdown (usually 3-7 days) where the dog cannot be touched legally.
 * Medical Hold: Hidden from the public website. The animal is in your custody but too sick/injured to be adopted.
(💡 Pro-Tip on Stray vs. Medical: If a dog is a stray AND hit by a car, which status wins? Usually, Stray Hold wins because it's a strict legal requirement. You would just add your is_medical_urgent Badge to let the vet know they are hurt).
2. The "Terminal" Statuses (The Closed Files)
These are the "End of the Road" statuses. When an animal hits one of these, the system should automatically lock the record and cancel any future automated tasks (like heartworm reminders or feeding tasks).
 * Adopted: Record archived. Ownership transferred to the adopter.
 * Transferred: Record archived. Ownership transferred to the partner rescue.
 * Deceased: Record archived.
Adding Deceased is an unfortunate but completely necessary reality of animal rescue. If you don't have this status, a dog that passes away from Parvo will sit in your system forever, and worse, a foster might accidentally get an automated text message a month later reminding them to give the dog a pill. Setting the status to "Deceased" must immediately sever all future automation.
