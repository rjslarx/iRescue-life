This is where iRescue.life transforms from a "planning tool" into an "operational necessity." You have handled the who (participants) and the what (manifest); now you need to handle the execution.
The logical flow moves from "Ready to Go" -> "In Transit" -> "The Handshake" -> "Data Settlement."
Here are the four essential features to complete the cycle, designed to save time and reduce liability.
1. The "Digital Load-In" (Departure Verification)
Transports are chaotic. It is surprisingly common for the wrong dog to get loaded, or for a dog to be left behind because they were hiding in the back of a kennel run.
 * The Feature: A "Load-In Checklist" on the Driver’s mobile view.
 * The Workflow:
   * The Driver opens the Manifest on their phone.
   * As they load each crate, they must tap "Loaded" next to the dog's name.
   * Crucial Gatekeeper: The system checks one last time: Does this dog have a PDF of their Health Cert (CVI) uploaded?
   * Blocker: If the CVI is missing, the app throws a RED ALERT and prevents the dog from being marked "Loaded" until the admin uploads the file. This prevents the transport from getting fined at state lines.
2. "Live Legs" Status Updates (En Route)
Google Chat is great for conversation ("We are stopping for gas"), but it’s bad for data ("Did Rex get his 2 PM seizure med?").
 * The Feature: An "Action Feed" for the trip.
 * The Workflow:
   * ETA Updates: Driver taps "Delayed 30 mins" button -> System auto-texts the receiving rescue contacts.
   * Medication & Care Log: If a dog is flagged as "Needs Meds," the driver gets a push notification at the due time. They tap "Administered," and it logs to the dog's medical record instantly.
   * Potty Breaks: Driver logs a "Walk Break." This calms the nerves of the receiving rescue, knowing the dogs are being cared for.
3. The "Custody Handshake" (The Transfer)
This is the most critical moment legally. The "Sender" is giving up ownership, and the "Receiver" is taking liability. Paper run sheets get lost, wet, or torn.
 * The Feature: QR Code Chain of Custody.
 * The Workflow:
   * The Driver arrives at the meeting point (e.g., a parking lot in Tennessee).
   * The Receiver (Rescue B) meets the truck.
   * The Scan: The Driver shows a QR Code on their phone (or the Receiver scans the dog's cage card).
   * The Signature: The Receiver signs on the Driver’s device (finger on glass).
   * The Result: The system records the precise GPS location and time of the transfer. The legal "Owner" field in the database automatically switches from Sender to Receiver.
4. The "One-Click" Data Import (The Closing)
Once the transport is marked "Complete," the administrative nightmare usually begins for the receiving rescue (typing in 20 new dogs).
 * The Feature: The "Ingest" Button.
 * The Workflow:
   * The Receiving Rescue gets a notification: "Transport Complete. 15 Animals waiting for import."
   * They review the list and click "Import to Roster."
   * The Magic: iRescue.life copies the photos, bio, microchip, and medical history into the Receiver's account. The Sender's record is archived as "Outcome: Transferred."
Replit Implementation Strategy
To make this manageable, I recommend building the "Mobile Driver View" first. This isn't a native app store app; it's just a responsive web page that looks like an app.
Prompt for Replit:
> "I need to build the 'Active Transport Mode' for the driver.
>  * UI: Create a mobile-first view for a specific TransportID.
>  * View Mode: It should list the stops in chronological order. Under each stop, list the dogs getting off.
>  * Action: Add a 'Confirm Dropoff' button next to each dog.
>  * Signature Pad: When 'Confirm Dropoff' is clicked, open a modal with a canvas for a digital signature.
>  * Logic: When signed and saved, update the database TransportManifestItem status to 'Delivered' and save the signature image URL."
>
