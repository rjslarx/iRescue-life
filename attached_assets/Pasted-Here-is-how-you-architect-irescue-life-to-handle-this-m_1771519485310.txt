Here is how you architect irescue.life to handle this multi-dimensional reality.

1. Decouple "Location" from "Adoption Status"
You must split the animal's state into independent data points.

Data Point 1: Location/Custody. (e.g., location_type: 'foster', foster_id: 123). This tracks where the dog sleeps.

Data Point 2: Adoption Status. (e.g., status: 'unavailable'). This determines if the public can see them on your website.

Data Point 3: Conditional Flags. (e.g., system_flags: ['medical_hold', 'hw_positive']). This tells the staff why the dog is unavailable.

2. The "Hard Stop" Adoption Guardrail
If an animal has a "Medical Hold" flag, your software should physically prevent a standard coordinator from clicking the "Finalize Adoption" button.

The Logic: You don't want a junior volunteer accidentally adopting out a dog that still has stitches in its abdomen.

The Override: The system should require a user with admin or medical_lead permissions to either remove the "Medical Hold" flag first, or click a special "Override Hold & Adopt" button (with a required text reason).

3. The Foster Portal UI (The "Special Instructions" Banner)
Since the foster parent is managing an animal with conditions, their "My Fosters" portal needs to adapt. If the dog has a medical_hold flag, the foster's dashboard should look different.

Red Warning Banner: At the top of the dog's profile in the foster app: "⚠️ MEDICAL HOLD: Do not allow this dog to run or jump. Leash walk only."

Prominent Meds: This ties perfectly into the medication charting we built earlier. The "To-Do" list for meds should be front and center.

Disabled Public Sharing: Normally, you want fosters to share their foster dog on Facebook. If the dog is on a legal or medical hold, the "Share Profile" button in their portal should be disabled or hidden.
