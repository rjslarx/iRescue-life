You should definitely make it an option during checkout, rather than a hardcoded requirement.
You are absolutely right to be concerned about this. Given the dense mosquito population and year-round humidity in the Gulf South and Louisiana, heartworm isn't just a possibility; it's an inevitability for unprotected dogs. Local rescues are completely justified in being hyper-focused on it.
However, from a software and operational standpoint, you have to draw a hard line between Heartworm Treatment (curing an active infection) and Heartworm Prevention (the monthly maintenance pill).
Here is how your software should handle both scenarios:
1. Active Heartworm Treatment (Mandatory Tracking)
If a dog is currently heartworm positive and undergoing treatment (e.g., the Fast Kill Immiticide injection protocol or the Slow Kill Doxycycline method), the rescue must track this post-adoption.
 * Why: The rescue is usually financially responsible for completing the treatment, and the dog's life is in immediate danger if the adopter doesn't bring them to their injection appointments or enforce strict crate rest.
 * Software Behavior: These should automatically populate in the Compliance Monitor during checkout, as we discussed previously.
2. Routine Heartworm Prevention (Optional Tracking)
If a dog is heartworm negative, tracking their monthly preventative pill for the next 10 to 15 years is an operational impossibility for a rescue.
 * Why: Rescue staff do not have the time to call 500 previous adopters every single month to ask if they gave their dog a piece of beef-flavored medicine. Legally, once the contract is signed, routine medical maintenance is the adopter's burden.
 * Software Behavior: You should give the rescue staff an "Add Custom Compliance Check" option during checkout.
The "6-Month Check-In" Strategy
Instead of trying to track every single monthly pill, the most effective tool rescues use in high-risk areas is a contractual 6-month proof of purchase.
During checkout, the staff can add a custom compliance task: "Provide vet records showing 6-month supply of Heartworm Prevention purchased." Set the due date for 6 months post-adoption. If the adopter uploads the receipt, the rescue knows the dog is safe, and they can close the file.
