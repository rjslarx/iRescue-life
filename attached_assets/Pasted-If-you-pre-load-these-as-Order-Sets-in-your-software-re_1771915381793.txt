1. The Giardia Protocol (The "5-Day Clear")
Giardia is a stubborn intestinal parasite. The danger is that dogs immediately reinfect themselves by licking their own fur where cysts are hiding, which is why a bath is medically required at the end of treatment.
Day 0: * Medication: Fenbendazole (Panacur) - Given SID (once daily) for 5 days.
Medication: Metronidazole - Given BID (twice daily) for 5 days to reduce diarrhea and intestinal inflammation.
Day 5: * Procedure: Medicated/Sanitary Bath - To wash the remaining cysts off the coat.
Day 14-21 (Offset 14): * Lab: Fecal Float / Giardia ELISA Recheck - To confirm the parasite is gone before moving the dog out of isolation.
2. The Demodex Protocol (Demodectic Mange)
Historically, this required toxic dips. Today, it is beautifully simple thanks to modern Isoxazoline preventatives, but it requires timeline tracking for re-check skin scrapes.
Day 0: * Lab: Skin Scrape (Initial Diagnosis).
Preventative: Bravecto (lasts 3 months) OR NexGard/Simparica (lasts 1 month). These flea/tick meds miraculously kill demodex mites.
Medication: Cephalexin or Amoxicillin (Given BID for 14 days) - Almost all mange dogs have secondary bacterial skin infections (pyoderma) from scratching.
Day 30 (Offset 30):
Lab: Skin Scrape Recheck #1.
Day 60 (Offset 60):
Lab: Skin Scrape Recheck #2 (Requires two negative scrapes to be officially cleared).
3. The Parvovirus Outpatient Protocol
Parvo is a viral infection with no cure. Treatment is entirely "supportive care" to keep the puppy alive while its immune system fights. Because IV fluids and hospitalization cost thousands, most rescues do "Outpatient/In-House" protocols. This is highly intensive and requires daily charting.
Day 0:
Lab: Parvo SNAP Test.
Exam/Procedure: STRICT ISOLATION PROTOCOL INITIATED.
Day 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 (Daily Tasks for 5+ Days):
Medication: Cerenia (Maropitant) - Anti-nausea, given SQ (injection) so they don't vomit it up.
Medication: Convenia (Antibiotic injection, lasts 14 days) OR daily Cefazolin - To prevent secondary sepsis, as the virus destroys the gut lining.
Procedure: Subcutaneous (SQ) Fluids - Given BID to prevent fatal dehydration.
Procedure: Syringe Feeding (e.g., Hill's a/d or Nutri-Cal) - Given QID (4x a day).
