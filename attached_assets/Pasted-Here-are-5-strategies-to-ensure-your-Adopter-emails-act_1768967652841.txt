Here are 5 strategies to ensure your Adopter emails actually get read.
1. The "Transactional" Subject Line (The Trojan Horse)
Don't write marketing subject lines. Write "Official Business" subject lines. People ignore "Newsletters," but they fear missing "Account Alerts."
• ❌ Weak: "Monthly update for Spot"
• ❌ Weak: "Heartworm reminder!"
• ✅ Strong: "ACTION REQUIRED: Medical Records for Spot"
• ✅ Strong: "Receipt: Your November Heartworm Prevention"
• ✅ Strong: "Alert: Vaccination Expiring in 3 Days"
Why it works: It triggers loss aversion. They open it because they think, "Uh oh, what is expiring?"
2. The "Pre-Header" text (The Second Subject Line)
In Gmail on a phone, the user sees the Subject Line (Bold) and the Snippet (Grey text). Most developers ignore the snippet, so it accidentally says "View this email in browser..." or "Unsubscribe..."
Fix: Hard-code a hidden "preview" span at the very top of your HTML email template.
<span style="display:none;font-size:0px;line-height:0px;">
  Spot needs his pill today to stay healthy. Tap to confirm...
</span>
3. The "Reply-to" Hack
Gmail's spam filter is aggressive. The single best signal to Google that "This is important" is if the user replies.
• The Tactic: End your automated email with a question.
• "P.S. How is Spot settling in? Reply with a photo—we'd love to see him!"
• The Result: When they reply "He's great!" and attach a pic, Gmail permanently whitelists your domain for that user. They will never miss a future email.
4. The "Automatic Bump" (The 48-Hour Rule)
Since sending is free, write a script for "Unopened" emails (using click-tracking as a proxy since Open tracking is broken).
• Trigger: If the user hasn't clicked the "Confirm Meds" button in 48 hours.
• Action: Send the exact same email but with a new subject line.
• Original: "Heartworm Reminder for Spot"
• Resend: "Did you see this? Spot's meds are due."
5. Focus on "Click Rate," not "Open Rate"
Since you can't trust Open Rates anymore, train your tenants to look at Clicks.
• Don't send an email that says "Give the pill."
• Send an email that says "Click here to log the pill."
• If they click, you know 100% they opened it, read it, and took action.
Here is the psychology behind why Push First, Email Second (or simultaneous) is the better workflow for an Adopter Portal.
1. The "Channel Psychology" (Why Push comes first)
• Push Notifications are "Interruptive" (Do it Now).  
• They appear on the lock screen. The user sees it, taps it, clicks "Confirm," and is done in 3 seconds. It is the path of least resistance.
• Emails are "Asynchronous" (Do it Later).  
• Emails get buried. Users often mentally categorize email as a "To-Do List" to check when they have time.
The Flaw in "Email First": If you send an email on the 1st and a push on the 3rd, you are wasting the "instant" power of the Push when the task is actually due. You are turning the Push into a "Nag" rather than a "Helper."
2. The Recommended Flow: "The Double Tap"
Since you have both channels for free, use them to reinforce each other, not as backups.
• Moment of Due Date (e.g., 9:00 AM):
• Action: Send BOTH (Push + Email).
• The Push: "💊 Time for Spot's heartworm pill! Tap to confirm." (Drives instant action).
• The Email: "Action Required: Monthly Prevention Due." (Serves as the persistent record/searchable receipt).
• 48 Hours Later (The Logic Check):
• Trigger: User has not clicked "Confirm."
• Action: Send Remider Email Only.
• Why Email here? If they ignored the Push on Day 1, it's gone from their lock screen. Sending another Push might feel like spamming. An email with the subject "OVERDUE: You missed Spot's pill" sits in their inbox staring at them until they delete it or act on it.
3. The "One-Click" Requirement (Deep Linking)
Whether it's Push or Email, the most critical success factor is where the link goes.
If the user clicks the notification and lands on the Home Page where they have to login -> find the dog -> find the records -> click confirm... they will quit.
You must implement Deep Links (or Magic Links):
• The URL: https://irescue.life/portal/confirm-meds?token=xyz123
• The Experience: They click the link -> It opens the PWA -> It instantly shows a modal: "Did you give Spot his pill?" -> [Yes] / [No].
• No Login: For this specific action, use a secure, one-time token so they don't even have to type their password.
