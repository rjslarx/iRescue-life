The "Impact Loop" is the secret weapon for donor retention. Most rescues lose monthly donors simply because the donor forgets what their money is actually doing. A donor shouldn't have to go hunting on Facebook to see if the rescue is still saving lives.
By automating this, you guarantee that every single active donor gets a "hit of dopamine" on the first of the month, right around the time their credit card is charged.
Here is how to architect this so that the shelter manager doesn't have to remember to do it, but the email still feels personal and current.
### The Automation Architecture
Instead of asking the shelter manager to write a new newsletter every month, your backend will dynamically generate the email by pulling the most recently adopted animal from their specific database.
**The Email Logic:**
 * **Trigger:** The 1st of every month at 9:00 AM.
 * **The Query:** Find the most recent animal where status = 'adopted' for that specific tenant.
 * **The Template:** *"Hey [Donor Name], your monthly donation just processed, and we wanted to show you exactly what it paid for. Thanks to you, [Animal Name] found their forever home this week. Here is a picture of them heading home. We literally couldn't do this without you."*
### The Replit Prompt for the Impact Loop
**Copy and paste this into Replit Agent:**
```text
I need to build an automated "Impact Loop" email system to increase retention for our monthly recurring donors. This requires setting up a scheduled cron job and an email compilation function.

1. Setup the Cron Job:
   - Install a scheduling library like `node-cron`.
   - Create a background task that runs on the 1st of every month at 9:00 AM (e.g., `0 9 1 * *`).

2. The Data Gathering Logic:
   - When the cron job fires, iterate through all active tenants in the database.
   - For each tenant, query the `animals` table to find the single most recently updated record where `status = 'adopted'`. Retrieve the animal's `name` and `photo_url`.
   - Query the database (or Stripe API) to get a list of all active monthly donors (names and email addresses) associated with that specific tenant.

3. The Email Generation & Sending:
   - Integrate an email sender (like Nodemailer using our platform SMTP, or Resend/SendGrid API).
   - For each active donor, construct a plain-text (or very lightweight HTML) email.
   - Subject: "Look who you saved this month 🐾"
   - Body: Dynamically insert the donor's name, the animal's name, and the animal's `photo_url`. 
   - Keep the tone conversational, thanking them for their active monthly support which made this adoption possible.
   - Send the emails asynchronously to avoid timing out the server.

```
### How to Avoid the Spam Folder (The SaaS Gotcha)
When you start sending automated emails on behalf of your tenants, deliverability is everything.
 1. **Plain Text is King:** Do not use massive, heavy HTML templates with a dozen columns and huge logos. Gmail and Apple Mail often route those straight to the "Promotions" tab. A simple text email with one single image attached feels like it came from a real human's outbox.
 2. **The "Reply-To" Header:** When you configure your email sender in the code, make sure the Reply-To address is set to the specific rescue's email address, not a generic noreply@irescue.life. If a donor replies to the email saying, "Aww, so cute!", that reply needs to go directly to the shelter manager. High reply rates train Google's algorithms that your automated emails are highly desired, keeping you out of the spam folder permanently.
